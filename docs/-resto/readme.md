# resto

![resto](readme.svg)

Discover opinionated Montreal restaurants ignored because of *bad bad reviews*—1–3★ rants where the reviewer is mad about everything except the food.

A 4.8★ spot tries to please everyone. A 3.9★ spot where someone leaves 1 star because *"service bête pis pas de parking, mais bon la bouffe est malade"* is probably worth a visit.

## goals

- **Discover opinionated gems:** surface Central Montreal restaurants (centered on Little Italy, ~1h walk radius + overshoot) whose Google Maps ratings are artificially depressed by unfair, misplaced, or non-food-related complaints.
- **Food first:** keep out non-food venues (bars, clubs, hotels, bowling alleys, dépanneurs) via the 3-way `ate` field and a small straggler skip list, veto bad-food/hygiene/decline spots, and highlight explicit food praise (`split`) as a top-tier badge rather than hard-dropping clean spots whose 1★ rants never mentioned the food.
- **Your taste, your rating:** one person's deal-breaker is another's bonus. Delivery drama and language friction don't matter to everyone (ignored by default), hard parking keeps the crowds away, a loud room is fun for some and a deal-breaker for others, while `price` counts against by default unless you're splurging. The user marks each complaint type as a plus, don't care, or minus, and the rating recomputes.
- **Recent first:** owners, chefs and menus change. Recent reviews weigh more, and reviews from before a detected change of hands barely count — without wiping out busy classics whose `lowest_rating` sample happens to skew older.
- **Simple, funny, safe, mobile-first:** lead with the funniest or most revealing bad-review quote, not a wall of analytics. Laugh at the situation, never at a person's identity, never name staff or reviewers, and never joke about someone who got hurt.
- **Signal over noise:** counteract rating inflation (anchoring, peer pressure, polite 5-star scores) by treating **1–3★ reviews** as higher-signal data. 5★ reviews are never read (often fake or empathy-based); Google's star counts only serve as the baseline the rating starts from.
- **Zero user profiling:** classify complaints purely from in-text tone and complaint type—no reviewer history needed. The user's taste settings live only in their own URL.
- **Trilingual UI (EN/FR/ES), verbatim quotes:** all controls switch EN/FR/ES via `<data-lang>en fr es</data-lang>` (native to `docs/_src/data-lang.js`), while review quotes stay verbatim in their original French, English, Spanish, or Franglais/Spanglish (auto-translation kills both the joke and the signal).
- **Offline batch, $0 ongoing:** run strictly as an offline, one-time batch pipeline **under $100 USD total** with **$0 recurring fees** (manual re-runs only if desired later).

## taxonomy: great bad reviews vs. real red flags

One shared tag set is used by the LLM classifier, the static JSON, and the UI.

### Complaint tags (the user can adjust each one)

Each tag is set to 👍 *plus*, 😐 *don't care*, or 👎 *minus* in the taste picker (see *Your rating* in Step 5). Default is 😐 *don't care* for all tags **except `price`**, which defaults to 👎 *minus* so overpriced spots don't get an automatic boost unless the user opts out. Delivery-app and OQLF/language complaints live under `friction` and are therefore ignored by default (😐).

| tag | default | EN label | FR label | ES label | what counts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `rude` | 😐 | Gruff service | Service bête | Trato seco | Blunt, cold or gruff staff, attitude, no smile, confronting/shaming over tips. |
| `slow` | 😐 | Long waits | Attente | Espera larga | Slow pacing, long waits for a table, the food or the bill, lineups. |
| `authentic` | 😐 | Too authentic | Trop authentique | Muy auténtico | Too spicy, unfamiliar traditional ingredients, small tasting portions, no substitutions, cash-only, no reservations, tiny opinionated menu/wine list. |
| `friction` | 😐 | Montreal friction | Cônes, parking & livraison | Fricción local | Delivery-app trouble (late driver, wrong address; voice-to-text *"Hubert"* = Uber Eats), OQLF / language-policy friction, street parking, orange cones/construction. Ignored by default. |
| `entitled` | 😐 | Main-character syndrome | Client roi froissé | Cliente exigente | Hyperbole over normal rules: waiting for full party, charging for extras, table time limits and turnover, no discount, no refund, prepayment or standard/disclosed tipping outrage (e.g. terminal prompts, disclosed large-group auto-grat), large-group drama, expecting free comps. |
| `loud` | 😐 | Loud & packed | Bruyant | Ruidoso | Noisy room, loud music, cramped tables, busy room. |
| `decor` | 😐 | Zero decor, drafty | Zéro déco, courants d'air | Sin decoración / frío | Fluorescent hole-in-the-wall aesthetics, uncomfortable chairs, cold seat by the door, winter drafts. |
| `price` | 👎 | Pricey | Trop cher | Caro | "Too expensive" with no food-quality complaint. Counts against by default. Shrinkflation goes to `decline`, tiny tasting portions to `authentic`, an empty/bad plate to `food`. |

### Derived badge (computed, not adjustable)

| tag | EN label | FR label | ES label | what counts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `split` | Food praised, rest hated | Bouffe top, reste bof | Comida top, resto mal | Computed locally: `food_sentiment = 1` (praised on this visit, never past-tense) and no red flag (*"service bête mais la bouffe est malade"*). Food praise never outweighs a red flag. |

### Red flags (fixed, always count against)

| tag | what counts |
| --- | --- |
| `food` | Core food failure: stale/spoiled ingredients, raw or overcooked mains, greasy/bland execution, obviously frozen/reheated dishes, bad/empty portions. Delivery-only food complaints count half: a soggy fry after 40 minutes in a car says little about the kitchen. |
| `hygiene` | Food poisoning, dirty kitchen/washrooms, or pests (heavy veto). |
| `decline` | Time-stamped deterioration (*"used to be amazing, not like before"*, obvious shrinkflation). Past-tense food praise in a `decline` review is `food_sentiment = -1`, never `1`. |
| `shady` | Billing or trust issues: wrong charges, undisclosed mandatory tips/fees, bait-and-switch, forcing tips on takeout/retail. |
| `serious` | Discrimination, harassment, allergy mishandled, accessibility. Never quoted, never filed as `rude` or `entitled`: not material for laughs. |

### Noise & non-complaints (ignored)

- `noise`: vague rants with no specifics (*"worst place ever"*), wrong business, never visited, or hours/closed confusion. Spread proportionally in the rating.
- `none`: polite 2–3★ reviews with no actual complaint (e.g., *"Très bon restaurant familial… On y retournera sûrement."*). Never forced into a complaint tag; left out of the complaint mix.

## methodology & data pipeline (verified 2026-11-22)

### Repo hygiene

Everything inside `docs/` is published live on GitHub Pages. Non-published datasets live in `data/resto/` at the repo root (gzip-compressed, zero API keys, zero reviewer PII). The classifier output holds keys and labels only, no review text. Only the compact final `docs/-resto/gems.json` (~150–300 KB) is committed inside `docs/`.

### Steps 1–3 — Extraction (COMPLETED, $32.11 total)

Executed directly via Outscraper's REST API (`https://api.outscraper.cloud`) instead of the mobile web UI (faster, zero manual borough picking, and unlocks `ignoreEmpty=true`).

- **Step 1 — Central Montreal directory (`$7.60`, 3,033 places incl. 10-place pilot):**
  - `POST /google-maps-search` across **48 Central Montreal FSAs** centered on Little Italy (`H2S`, `H2G`, `H2R`, `H2P`, `H2E`, `H3N`, `H1Y`, `H1X`, `H1T`, `H2A`, `H1Z`, `H2M`, `H2N`, `H2C`, `H2B`, `H3L`, `H2T`, `H2J`, `H2H`, `H2W`, `H2X`, `H2V`, `H2K`, `H2L`, `H1W`, `H1V`, `H3P`, `H3R`, `H4P`, `H3S`, `H3T`, `H3V`, `H3W`, `H3X`, `H4A`, `H3Y`, `H3Z`, `H3A`, `H3B`, `H3G`, `H3H`, `H2Y`, `H2Z`, `H3C`, `H3J`, `H3K`, `H4C`, `H4G`).
  - Parameters: `limit=250`, `dropDuplicates=true`, `filters=["operational_only"]`, `language="en"`, `region="CA"`, no enrichments.
  - Result: **3,023 unique places**, saved cleaned to `data/resto/central_mtl_places.json.gz`.
- **Step 2 — Local pre-filter (`$0`):**
  - Kept Montreal `H*` postal codes within **6.5 km** of Little Italy (`45.5361, -73.6149`).
  - Excluded non-restaurant Google types (groceries, markets, dépanneurs, caterers, coffee shops, bakeries, dessert shops, bars without restaurant) and multi-location chains (`McDonald's`, `Tim Hortons`, `A&W`, `Subway`, `Pizza Hut`, `Boustan`, `St-Hubert`, `L'Œufrier`, `Mito/Aki/Bento Sushi`, etc.).
  - Filtered to overall rating **3.4–4.4★**, **≥ 30** total reviews, and **≥ 8** low-star (`1–3★`) reviews using `reviews_per_score`.
  - Result: **602 clean independent restaurants**.
- **Step 3 — Targeted 1–3★ review scrape (`$24.51`, 11,497 units incl. pilot):**
  - `GET /google-maps-reviews` in resumable chunks of 50 `place_id`s: `reviewsLimit=20`, `sort=lowest_rating`, `cutoffRating=3`, `ignoreEmpty=true`, `language=fr`, `region=CA`.
  - Result: **11,447 non-empty original-language reviews** (`100%` have text; `10,434` 1★, `635` 2★, `378` 3★; ~69% French, ~31% English), saved cleaned to `data/resto/central_mtl_reviews.json.gz`.

**Verified Outscraper API quirks (keep for any future re-run):**

- **Query phrasing trap:** `"restaurant, <FSA>, Quebec, Canada"` leaks Quebec City (`G1*`) results. Always include the city: `"restaurant, Montreal, QC <FSA>"` (or `Westmount` / `Mount Royal` / `Outremont`).
- **Single-call bilingual extraction:** Outscraper never auto-translates `review_text`. A single `language=fr&region=CA` call returns both French and English reviews verbatim in their original language (verified via overlap check—saves paying twice).
- **`ignoreEmpty=true` is API-only** (missing in the mobile web UI) and worked 100%.
- **No structured sub-ratings:** `review_questions` (Food / Service / Atmosphere) returned `null` on `11,447/11,447` reviews. Classification must rely on review text, `stars`, `date`, and `reviews_per_score`.
- **No combining `cutoff` + `cutoffRating`:** Outscraper's date `cutoff` forces `sort=newest`, overriding `lowest_rating`. Always handle recency locally.

### Sampling caveats (diagnostic checks, 2026-11-22)

- **Mostly a 1★ sample (with a size bias on 2–3★):** Outscraper fills the 20 slots from the lowest rating up. 91% of sampled reviews are 1★, while 1★ is only 41% of the real 1–3★ reviews; **436 of 602 places** have nothing but 1★ in their sample (median **739** total reviews), while the **166 places** with any 2–3★ sampled are 4.5× smaller (median **163** reviews). Do **not** blindly project small-place 2–3★ city priors onto busy 1★-only places (see *Complaint mix*).
- **Not ordered by date:** within a star level, 42% of neighbouring reviews are out of date order. Use `date`, never the position.
- **Old samples don't mean dead restaurants:** 86 places have a median review year ≤ 2021, and a harsh 12-month half-life + `sum(w) < 4` veto would drop **216 of 602 places** (36%), including busy classics like Pachamama (1,310 reviews), Taza Flores (562), and Fiorellino (2,533), simply because Google's `lowest_rating` sort surfaces popular older rants. Use a gentler decay and badge older samples instead of vetoing them.
- **What people complain about (rough keyword counts):** ~26% mention price, ~26% waits or minutes, 7.1% delivery (304 places), 5.3% tipping (306 places — half the dataset!), 2.2% language/OQLF (169 places), 1.1% parking, 0.1% cones/construction; ~4.2% (~484 reviews) are in Spanish. Québécois slang is rare (*écoeurant* 18, *cochon* 7, *pas pire* 5): sarcasm, past-tense praise (*"avant c'était bon"*), and mixed reviews are the real classification risk.
- **Non-food stragglers:** a few hotels (`Hotel de Paris`, `Hôtel Monville`), bowling alleys (`Darling Bowling`), convenience stores (`KaleMart24`), dessert bars (`Lokma`, `Delysées`), and pure bars (`Invisible Bar`, `Pirandello Sport Bar`, `Bar Brewskis`, `Bar de la Salle`…) carry the Google `Restaurant` type. Handle via the 3-way `ate` enum and a small pre-skip list in Step 4/5 without rewriting `central_mtl_reviews.json.gz`.
- **Not everything is funny:** ~80 reviews mention racism or discrimination, ~80 mention allergies, and some name individual staff members (*"Service médiocre par Juliette"*). Hence the `serious` red flag and strict quote safety checks.
- **Review keys:** reviews carry no ID. A review's key is `place_id:index`, its position in `central_mtl_reviews.json.gz`. Never re-sort or rewrite that file.

### Step 4 — Batch LLM classification (NEXT, est. $0.50–$1.50)

Run the reviews in `data/resto/central_mtl_reviews.json.gz` (~1.5M input tokens + ~0.8M output tokens, skipping the short non-restaurant straggler list) through an offline batch job (e.g. Gemini Flash-Lite Batch API). The API key comes from an environment variable and never enters the repo.

**Before the full batch:**

1. **Gold set (ready):** `data/resto/gold_set.json` (generated by `data/resto/make_gold_set.py`) contains **60 stratified reviews across 60 distinct places** (FR, EN, ES; 1/2/3★; Québécois slang, sarcasm, hygiene + food praise, past-tense praise, change of hands, tipping, delivery, OQLF/language, bars/hotels vs. walk-outs, staff-named reviews, `serious` red flags, polite 2–3★, noise, and random baseline). Owner hand-labels `gold` before the pilot.
2. **Pilot:** run 2 prompt variants on the 60 gold reviews plus ~200 more, compare against `gold`, fix the rules, then run everything. The run costs about a dollar; redoing it is the real cost.
3. **Quote check in code:** every `quote` must be an exact substring of its review (after whitespace normalization), contain **no personal names** (staff or reviewer), and come only from reviews with `red_flags == []`. Models quietly fix typos and accents, and the typos are part of the charm. Retry or set `null` otherwise.
4. **Set-up:** structured (JSON-schema) output, temperature 0. Give the model the place name and cuisine (context: *too spicy* at a Sichuan place) but not its rating. Save the model name, prompt version, date and raw responses with the results, so re-scoring never needs a re-run.
5. **Review text is data:** it is untrusted input, never instructions to the model.

**Mandatory prompt rules (calibrated from our local scan of the real reviews):**

- **Québécois false friends:** *"écoeurant"*, *"malade"* (about food, e.g. *"la bouffe est malade"*), *"cochon"*, *"terrible"* (positive slang), and *"pas pire"* mean **delicious / good**—NOT food poisoning! Conversely, *"tombé malade" / "rendu malade" / "été malade"* IS a `hygiene` red flag. *"Service bête"* means gruff/rude service (`rude`).
- **Present-tense food praise only:** `food_sentiment = 1` means the reviewer praised the food **on this visit**. Past-tense praise (*"Auparavant la meilleure pizza… maintenant c'est fini"*, *"used to be good"*) is `food_sentiment = -1` + `decline`, never `1`.
- **Sarcasm & negation:** do NOT treat sarcastic praise as positive food sentiment (real examples: *"j'ai reçu une véritable brique. Franchement, excellent choix si vous planifiez des rénovations"* → `food` red flag, not `split`; *"The best sushis are those without fish... The fish wasn't fresh"* → `food` red flag).
- **Food praise next to a red flag stays a red flag:** *"Attendez-vous à dîner avec une souris… Certes la nourriture est bonne"* → `hygiene`, with `food_sentiment = 1` (never `split`, never quoted).
- **Tipping boundary:** standard terminal tip prompts or disclosed large-group auto-gratuity → `entitled`; undisclosed mandatory tips/fees or forcing tips on takeout/retail → `shady`; server confronting/shaming a diner over tip amount → `rude` (or `shady` if forced).
- **Montreal quirks seen in our data:** voice-to-text `"Hubert"` = Uber Eats (`friction`, `channel = "delivery"`); OQLF / language-policy complaints (`friction`); large-group (10–13 people) complaints and prepayment outrage (`entitled`); winter door-draft complaints (`decor`).
- **Languages:** classify reviews in any language (FR, EN, ES, etc.). Verbatim `quote` is allowed in **French, English, Spanish, or Franglais/Spanglish**; set `quote = null` for non-Latin scripts.
- **`serious` before `rude`:** discrimination, harassment, an allergy mishandled or an accessibility problem is `serious`, never `rude` or `entitled`.
- **Price vs food:** "too expensive" alone is `price`; "too expensive for an empty/bad plate" is `food`; "same price, smaller portions than before" is `decline`.
- **Safe & fun quote rules:** verbatim excerpt ≤ 180 chars in FR/EN/ES, the funniest or most revealing line (prefer sharp contrast, absurd hyperbole, or self-owns); **zero personal names** of staff or reviewers (skip or set `null` if a funny line names a person); no slurs, remarks about anyone's origin, or unverified legal accusations; `null` if any red flag is present or nothing fits.

**Per-review JSON output schema (flat & cheap).** The model reports observations; judgments are computed locally, so they can be re-tuned for $0:

```json
{
  "primary": "slow",
  "complaints": ["slow", "rude"],
  "red_flags": [],
  "channel": "dine_in",
  "ate": "ate",
  "change": "none",
  "food_sentiment": 1,
  "tone": "reluctant_praise",
  "quotable": 3,
  "quote": "Service bête mais la bouffe est malade."
}
```

- `primary`: the one main reason for the low stars (a complaint tag, a red flag, `noise`, or `none` for polite 2–3★ reviews with no complaint)
- `complaints`: complaint tags; `red_flags`: red-flag tags, `serious` included
- `channel`: `dine_in`, `takeout`, `delivery` or `unknown`
- `ate`: `ate` (ate or ordered food), `walked_out_or_turned_away` (restaurant visit where they left or were refused seating before eating), or `non_food_venue` (bar/drinks/bouncer/hotel/bowling/retail)
- `change`: `none`, `better`, `worse`, or `neutral` — strictly explicit mention of a new owner, management, chef or menu (never inferred from `decline` alone)
- `food_sentiment`: `-1` (bad on this visit), `0` (not mentioned / neutral), `1` (praised on this visit — never past-tense)
- `tone`: `plain`, `reluctant_praise`, `self_own`, `unhinged_owner`, `overdramatic`, or `absurd_reason` (helps quote selection and *Les Chialeux d'or* awards)
- `quotable`: `0` (dull) to `3` (genuinely funny or iconic complaint, safe to display)
- `quote`: verbatim excerpt ≤ 180 chars in FR/EN/ES with no personal names, or `null`
- The key `id` (`place_id:index`) is added locally. No `verdict` (it follows from the tags) and no `split` (derived locally).

**Decision point — buy more data?** The premise ignores 5★ reviews; the main gaps are (a) busy classics whose 20-review `lowest_rating` sample skews old (≤ 2021) and (b) 2–4★ reviews where honest food praise lives. Don't buy before the pilot. After classification, check:

- how many strong places have old samples or zero sampled 2–3★ reviews;
- whether the 2★ and 3★ complaint mixes differ much from 1★.

If buying: for the ~250 finalists only, the newest ~40 reviews of any star (`sort=newest`), dropping 5★ text on arrival. That brings fresh 2025–2026 reviews, honest 2–4★ food praise, and a recent-trend signal (a jump in recent 1–3★ share flags a change of hands). At the rate paid so far ($2.13 per 1,000 reviews), about $21. First check whether the API can skip 5★ reviews, and dedupe against the existing sample by text + date.

**Optional verification pass:** with ~$68 left under the $100 cap, run a stronger LLM on the top ~150–250 finalists to double-check sarcasm and Québécois/Spanish nuance, verify no personal names slipped into quotes, and pick each place's funniest quote by comparing candidates side by side (more reliable than the absolute `quotable` score).

### Step 5 — Local scoring & export (NEXT, $0, freely re-tunable)

**Recency (gentle decay — recent weighs more, classics survive):**

- Weight `w = max(0.2, 0.5 ^ (age_in_months / 24))` counted from the snapshot (`2026-11`), tunable: 2 years old → 0.5, 4 years → 0.25, older → 0.2 floor. Because `sort=lowest_rating` is not chronological, a harsh 12-month decay + `sum(w) < 4` veto would wipe out 36% of restaurants (including Pachamama, Taza Flores, and Fiorellino). Instead, flag places with few post-2022 sampled reviews as *"older sample"* and shrink their mix more strongly.
- **Change of hands:** triggered strictly when `≥ 2` reviews have `change != "none"` (never inferred from `decline` alone). Anchor to the **most recent cluster** (earliest date inside the latest 18-month cluster of `change` mentions, not the global earliest date across a decade). Reviews before that date get a further `× 0.1`, and the card shows *changed hands ~YYYY* only when explicit `change` triggered it.
- `hygiene` and `serious` flags from the last 12 months get `1.5×` penalty weight.

**Hard drop rules (veto):**

- explicit non-restaurant straggler skip list (`Hotel de Paris`, `Hôtel Monville`, `Darling Bowling`, `KaleMart24`, `Lokma Bar à Desserts`, `Delysées Desserts`, etc.) or majority `ate = "non_food_venue"` (bars, clubs, venues);
- `≥ 2` recent `hygiene` or `serious` flags (or any corroborated poisoning/pest report);
- weighted `food` red-flag share among the worst ~30% of places (calibrated from the post-classification distribution; delivery-only `food` flags count half);
- `≥ 2` `decline` flags in the last 18 months.

*(Explicit food praise — `food_sentiment = 1` with no red flag → `split` — is a strong badge, ranking boost, and optional UI filter rather than a hard veto, so clean high-`ate` restaurants whose 1★ rants didn't mention food aren't thrown away.)*

**Complaint mix (per place, per star level):**

- Each review gets one bucket: its most severe red flag if any (`serious` > `hygiene` > `food` > `shady` > `decline`), else its `primary` complaint. `noise` and `none` are left out (spread proportionally).
- For each star level with sampled reviews, compute the recency-weighted share of each bucket, shrunk lightly toward the city-wide share at that level (~5 pseudo-reviews).
- Because the 166 places with sampled 2–3★ reviews are 4.5× smaller than the 436 busy 1★-only places, do **not** overwrite busy places' 2–3★ buckets with small-place priors: extrapolate conservatively from each place's own 1★ mix (damped toward city 1★) and note on *the bill* when it is estimated from 1★ reviews.
- Share × the place's real count at that level = the estimated number of 1★/2★/3★ reviews per bucket. This feeds *the bill* and *your rating*.

**Your rating (headline, computed in the browser):** start from Google's star counts (5★ counted, never read). Then, per complaint bucket and the user's setting:

- 😐 *don't care* (default for all complaint tags except `price`, including delivery/language under `friction`): those reviews are removed—*sans le chialage*;
- 👍 *plus*: their complaint is your bonus, so they count as 5★;
- 👎 *minus* (default for `price`): they count as given.

Red flags always count as given. Example: Mam Grillades has 107/10/33/79/318 reviews at 1★…5★ (Google 3.9★). Removing just 80% of its 107 1★ reviews already gives 4.4★. Default sort is **Your rating** (or **Closest** when user location is enabled), with **Gap** (*your rating* − Google rating) available as a sort option so messy 3.5★ spots don't outrank spotless 4.3★→4.7★ culinary gems. It's a generous estimate: 4–5★ reviews are never checked.

**Quote safety check in code (Step 5):** verify every selected quote is a verbatim substring of its source review, comes from a `red_flags == []` review, is in FR/EN/ES, is ≤ 180 chars, and contains no personal names (regex guard for `par <Name>`, waiter/waitress/manager names, plus manual/Pass-2 check).

**Early sanity-check benchmarks from our local peek** (hypotheses, not targets: a keyword check found food words in 90% of Fiorellino's and 95% of Piatto Pieno's sampled 1★ reviews):

- *Expected winners:* **Mam Grillades** (3.9★, 0.5 km — slow waits & gruff counter, zero food complaints), **Restaurant Pachamama** (4.2★, 0.6 km — cold seat by the door in winter, *"la bouffe n'est pas pire"*), **ALMA** (4.4★, 1.7 km — tasting-menu prepayment/stiff-service shock), **Taza Flores** (4.4★, 1.9 km), **Fiorellino** (4.1★ — 55-min table turnover rage).
- *Expected vetoes / cautions:* **Meli-Melo Market** (3.8★) and **Resto Los Planes** (4.3★) — each had an attractive service/wait quote, but multiple recent food-quality red flags; **Bistro Chez Alberto** (3.5★) — hilarious service/VLT drama, but has a billing-dispute (`shady`) flag to weigh.

## static dataset schema (`docs/-resto/gems.json`)

Compact, privacy-clean file consumed directly by `index.html`:

```json
{
  "generated": "2026-11",
  "area": "Montréal (Central)",
  "center": [45.5361, -73.6149],
  "items": [
    {
      "id": "ChIJ...",
      "name": "Chez Exemple",
      "hood": "Petite-Italie",
      "cuisine": "Bistro",
      "lat": 45.536,
      "lng": -73.615,
      "km": 0.5,
      "rating": 3.9,
      "stars": [107, 10, 33, 79, 318],
      "mix": { "slow": [45, 3, 10], "rude": [40, 2, 6], "decor": [8, 0, 0], "price": [0, 2, 7], "food": [14, 3, 10] },
      "analyzed": 20,
      "sample_stars": [20, 0, 0],
      "older_sample": false,
      "food_praised": 6,
      "changed": null,
      "tags": ["split", "slow", "rude"],
      "quotes": [
        { "stars": 1, "date": "2026-04", "lang": "fr", "tone": "reluctant_praise", "tags": ["rude", "split"], "text": "Service bête mais la bouffe est malade." }
      ],
      "maps": "https://maps.google.com/?cid=..."
    }
  ]
}
```

- `stars`: Google's counts at 1★…5★ (`reviews_per_score`), the base of *your rating*
- `mix`: estimated number of 1★/2★/3★ reviews per bucket (complaint tag or red flag), recency-weighted; the page computes *your rating* and *the bill* from it
- `sample_stars`: how many 1★/2★/3★ reviews were actually sampled (so *the bill* can note when it is estimated from 1★ only)
- `older_sample`: `true` when most sampled reviews predate 2022
- `changed`: estimated change-of-hands month (`YYYY-MM`, strictly from explicit `change` mentions) or `null`

## URL state (freeze before v1)

- The hash holds names, never list positions: place IDs (`ChIJ…`), tag names with their setting (e.g. `friction+ entitled+ price=`), sort and filters, under `#en/…`, `#fr/…`, or `#es/…`.
- Unknown tags or IDs are ignored on load, so a regenerated `gems.json` or a new tag never breaks an old link. A place that left the list shows a short *no longer on the list* note.
- Once published, the format can't change (`AGENTS.md`): a breaking change means a new folder.

## humour & voice

- **Safe and fun:** laugh at the situation, never at a person's identity, never name staff or reviewers, and never joke about someone who got hurt. `serious` and red-flag reviews are never quoted.
- **The headline joke is the rating:** *« Google : 3,9★ — sans le chialage : 4,4★ »*.
- **Already in v1:** the bill, the bold pivot (*mais / but / pero*), survival tips, complaint bingo, the glossary.
- **Later — awards (*Les Chialeux d'or*):** powered by tags + `tone`, e.g. *Longest wait for the best food*, *Coldest seat by the door*, *Most outraged by a reasonable policy*, *"How is this 4.3★?!" truther*, *Legendary gruff owner*.

## privacy & attribution hygiene

- Strip all reviewer names, profile links, user IDs, and review IDs from both `data/resto/` and `docs/-resto/gems.json`.
- Truncate timestamps to `YYYY-MM`.
- In the published `docs/-resto/gems.json`, keep quotes to short verbatim excerpts (≤ 180 chars) in FR/EN/ES, never full review dumps.
- Quotes are checked verbatim in code, **never name staff or reviewers**, and never come from a review with a red flag (`serious` included).
- `data/resto/classified_reviews.json.gz` holds keys and labels only, no review text.
- Link straight to Google Maps for live status, hours, menus, and full reviews.
- Credit line on the page: *Excerpts from Google Maps reviews // Extraits d'avis Google Maps // Extractos de reseñas de Google Maps*, with the link to each place.
- Stay non-commercial: no ads, no paid tier. Handle removal requests (see `data/resto/readme.md`) promptly.
